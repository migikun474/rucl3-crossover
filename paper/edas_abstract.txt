Claims of quantum advantage for simulating materials are usually made for toy models at an arbitrary accuracy. We ask the question for a real material and an accuracy fixed by experiment: the dynamical structure factor S(q,ω) of the Kitaev magnet α-RuCl3, resolved to the energy resolution of inelastic neutron scattering. We measure the cost of exact classical simulation on clusters of up to 18 spins and of matrix-product-state time evolution on cylinders, measure the Trotter error of the corresponding quantum circuits against the exact results, and feed the logical circuits to a surface-code resource estimator. Across three published exchange models, a fault-tolerant quantum computer overtakes exact state-vector simulation at N* ≈ 36–38 spins. For the Winter et al. model it needs 0.25 M (1.7 M) physical qubits at a physical error rate of 10^-4 (10^-3), and at the crossover both machines need about a century, so the crossover marks where neither is practical rather than where quantum simulation becomes useful. Preparing the ground state from the best product state moves the crossover to N* ≈ 92–94. Even the qubit-minimal layout needs 29–34 thousand physical qubits at p = 10^-4, 29–34 times the 1000-qubit target of India’s National Quantum Mission, and the two-qubit error reported for the 25-qubit QpiAI-Indus system is 4 times above the surface-code threshold. We claim no advantage today; every projection is stated with its assumptions, and code and data will be made public.
